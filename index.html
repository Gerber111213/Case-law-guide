⚖ Mandatory Charter Cautions & Demand Readouts

1. INVESTIGATIVE DETENTION (Charter s. 10(a) & 10(b) — R. v. Mann / R. v. Suberu)

"You are being detained for an investigation into [state specific offence/reason]. You have the right to retain and instruct counsel without delay. You may call any lawyer you want. There is a 24-hour telephone service available which provides legal advice without charge and can explain the Legal Aid plan to you. Do you understand? Do you wish to call a lawyer now?"

2. ARREST CAUTION & RIGHT TO COUNSEL (Charter s. 10(a) & 10(b))

"You are under arrest for [state offence]. It is my duty to inform you that you have the right to retain and instruct counsel without delay. You may call any lawyer you want. A free 24-hour Legal Aid duty counsel service is available to provide immediate legal advice. Do you understand? Do you want to call a lawyer?"

3. PRIMARY POLICE CAUTION (Right to Silence — R. v. Hebert)

"You are charged with / will be charged with [offence]. Do you wish to say anything in answer to the charge? You are not obliged to say anything unless you wish to do so, but whatever you say may be given in evidence."

4. SECONDARY CAUTION (Prior Statement / Inducement Erasure)

"If you have spoken to any police officer or to anyone with authority or if any such person has spoken to you in connection with this matter, I want it clearly understood that I do not want it to influence you in making any statement."

5. MANDATORY ALCOHOL SCREENING (MAS) DEMAND — CCC s. 320.27(2)

"I demand that you provide forthwith a sample of your breath into an approved screening device to enable a proper analysis to be made to determine the presence, if any, of alcohol in your blood, and to accompany me for that purpose."

Note: Right to counsel (s. 10(b)) is constitutionally suspended for valid forthwith roadside ASD demands (R. v. Orbanski; R. v. Elias; R. v. Thomsen).

📶 Canadian Legal Thresholds & Grounds Ladder

TIER 1: MERE HUNCH / CONSENSUAL INTERACTION (Zero Detention)

Police may approach and ask questions in public spaces without grounds, provided a reasonable person would feel free to walk away (R. v. Suberu). Zero power to compel ID (outside licensed driving/regulatory duties) or search.

TIER 2: STATUTORY TRAFFIC STOP / MAS (TSA s. 166 / CCC s. 320.27(2))

Random traffic stop authorized for highway safety regulation (R. v. Ladouceur). Permits demand for DL, registration, insurance, mechanical check, and forthwith ASD breath sample if device is immediately on hand. Cannot morph into a criminal fishing expedition without new grounds (R. v. Mellenthin).

TIER 3: REASONABLE SUSPICION (Articulable Cause — R. v. Mann / R. v. Chehil)

Constellation of objectively discernible facts showing a reasonable possibility (not probability) that the individual is implicated in a specific criminal offence. Authorizes brief investigative detention, s. 10(a)/(b) rights, and protective safety pat-down ONLY if reasonable safety concern exists.

TIER 4: REASONABLE & PROBABLE GROUNDS (RPG — R. v. Storrey)

Subjective belief honestly held by the officer AND objectively justifiable by a reasonable person standing in the officer's shoes (credibly-based probability). Required for warrantless arrest (CCC s. 495), breathalyzer demand (s. 320.28), and search warrants (s. 487).

TIER 5: EXIGENT CIRCUMSTANCES + RPG (CCC s. 529.3 / s. 117.02 / s. 487.11)

RPG to arrest/search PLUS urgency making obtaining a warrant impracticable due to imminent bodily harm/death or imminent loss/destruction of evidence. Convenience or administrative delay is NEVER exigency.
